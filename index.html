About

I build AI and software that has to be trusted

I am an AI product manager who ships fast and reads the market closely. My edge is building AI that has to be trustworthy in regulated domains, where a wrong answer is not just a bug, it is a legal or audit problem.

Trustworthy AI, by design

I build the evaluation layer, the guardrails, and the human-in-the-loop review that let automated systems stand up to auditors. Compliance across WCAG, Section 508, HIPAA, FERPA, COPPA, and PCI-DSS.

Owns delivery end to end

From discovery and the PRD to sprints, launch, and the metrics afterward. Comfortable as the product manager, the program manager running several launches at once, and the project manager keeping a scope on track.

Reads the market, ships what matters

About ten years across health tech, ed-tech, and SaaS. I have owned roadmaps, payments and vendor integrations, revenue operations, and 0-to-1 products, and I keep the numbers honest.

At DigitalNet.ai I own an automated accessibility conformance platform end to end. It checks websites, codebases, and documents against WCAG 2.1 and 2.2, PDF/UA, and Section 508, and its output carries real legal and audit weight, so I keep it honest: it measures what it claims, escalates to a human when it should, and stands up when an auditor asks how it got there. That standard runs through my whole career, from student data under FERPA and COPPA to patient records under HIPAA and payments under PCI-DSS.

How I work

Across product, program, and project

Three lenses on the same job: get the right thing built and shipped. Here is what each looks like when I run it.

Product management

Deciding what to build, why, and how we will know it worked.

  • Discovery: user interviews, sales calls, surveys, and product analytics.
  • Definition: a PRD with one success metric, its guardrails, and clear non-goals.
  • Prioritization with RICE, a groomed backlog, and Jira stories with acceptance criteria.
  • Launch, then read the metric tree and iterate.

Project management

Getting a committed scope delivered without surprises.

  • Two-week sprints: planning, standups, refinement, demo, retro.
  • Planning poker so estimates are owned by the team.
  • Clean hand-offs: design to engineering to QA to my UAT sign-off.
  • Scope control and a RAID log so risks do not become fires.

Program management

Running several launches at once so they add up, not collide.

  • Map cross-team dependencies and sequence the work.
  • A release calendar so QA and marketing are not overloaded.
  • Weekly red, amber, green status to leadership with the decisions I need.
  • Keep finance, legal, and vendors aligned so nothing stalls late.

DigitalNet.ai

AI Product Manager Jul 2026 to present · Remote

Context

DigitalNet.ai builds an agentic AI platform for compliance. I own the automated accessibility conformance product, where a false "this is accessible" is a legal exposure, not a cosmetic miss.

What I own

  • The roadmap for a platform that checks websites, codebases, and documents against WCAG 2.1 and 2.2, PDF/UA, and Section 508.
  • The evaluation layer that proves the AI measures what it claims, and the human-in-the-loop handoff for ambiguous cases.
  • Audit-readiness: every result traceable back to the rule it tested and the evidence behind it.

How I work it

  • Turn each conformance rule into a testable check with a clear pass, fail, or needs-review outcome.
  • Build an eval harness that scores the agent against expert-reviewed ground truth, so we can trust the automation.
  • Design escalation so low-confidence findings go to a human reviewer instead of being guessed.

Guardrails

What I watch: false-pass rate above all (a wrong "accessible" is the costly error), agreement with expert audits, and traceability so an auditor can see how any result was produced.

STC Health

Senior Product Manager Jan 2025 to Oct 2025

Context

Public-health SaaS for state immunization and registry programs. Onboarding a new state was slow, finance had no clean cost view, and duplicate patient records kept creeping in.

What I owned

  • The modernization roadmap into one compliance-ready cloud platform across 16 U.S. states, built for HIPAA.
  • Patient data quality, the finance and cost-center view, and vendor and supply-chain integration.

How I worked it

  • Replaced manual, per-state setup with templated onboarding and bulk import with validation.
  • Moved patient deduplication from rigid rules to an ML record-matching model, with a tokenized patient-search system so lookups stayed fast and PHI stayed protected.
  • Sent low-confidence matches to a human, so the model never silently merged two different patients.

Impact

  • 16 U.S. states
  • $1.8M+ annual savings
  • 30% faster onboarding
  • 37% efficiency gain
Guardrails: data error rate and false-merge rate, plus zero HIPAA audit findings on the new flow. confirm duplicate-reduction number
