About

Building AI that has to be trusted

I am an AI product manager who ships fast and reads the market closely. At DigitalNet.ai I own an automated accessibility conformance platform end to end. It checks websites, codebases, and documents against WCAG 2.1 and 2.2, PDF/UA, and Section 508, and its output carries real legal and audit weight, so I keep it honest: it measures what it claims, escalates to a human when it should, and stands up when an auditor asks how it got there.

That standard runs through my whole career. I have built AI and software that has to be trusted in regulated places: student data under FERPA and COPPA, patient records under HIPAA, and payments under PCI-DSS. Across about ten years in health tech, ed-tech, and SaaS, the through line stays the same: read the market, ship what matters, and build products people can trust.

How I work

Across product, program, and project

Three lenses on the same job: get the right thing built and shipped. Here is what each looks like in my day to day.

Product management

I decide what to build and why. Discovery through user interviews and analytics, a PRD with one success metric and its guardrails, prioritization with RICE, and Jira stories the team can actually build. After launch I watch the numbers and iterate.

Project management

I get a committed scope delivered without surprises. Two-week sprints, planning poker, a groomed backlog, and clean hand-offs from design to engineering to QA to a UAT sign-off. I protect the sprint goal and keep a RAID list so risks do not become fires.

Program management

I run several launches at once so they add up instead of colliding. I map cross-team dependencies, keep a release calendar, and send leadership a weekly red, amber, green status with the numbers, the risks, and the decisions I need.
